## The organizational need

**LinuxONE needed a dedicated design and research practice.** The platform had engineering and product management, but no team whose job was to find out what clients could not do and to design the response. I founded that practice and grew it from **zero to six people**, creating capacity for research and design across LinuxONE and Linux on Z.

**Hiring was only part of it.** A new practice also needs a reason for engineering and product management to involve it early. The council described below gave the practice something those teams could not get on their own: a standing group of clients who would answer questions before a roadmap was fixed.

## My decisions

**I established recurring client participation.** I founded the LinuxONE client council with about **30 clients**, recurring virtual sessions, and one in-person event each year. Its purpose is design input and future direction, not sales, and the sessions are run that way.

**I connected the practice to product definition.** I lead design and research on Launchpad for LinuxONE and do product leadership work without the product manager title, with influence across product management, strategy, and engineering. The council's findings are part of how that product was scoped.

## Team activity and evidence

**The practice supports ongoing research and design, and the council provides recurring client input.** Team members run sessions, synthesize findings, and present their own work. I established the practice and council and lead the product and platform direction described here.

**The evidence is organizational and qualitative.** Practice size and recurring client participation show what was established. This describes an organizational capability rather than a product with its own telemetry.
