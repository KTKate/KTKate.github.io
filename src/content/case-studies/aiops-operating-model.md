## What I inherited

**The team was not ineffective because of the people in it.** Nine designers and researchers were spread across a portfolio of observability and analytics products for IT operations on IBM Z. They were busy. The problem was that much of the work was not connected to anything a product needed, so product management and engineering had stopped relying on it.

**A team that is not needed gets ignored, and a team that is ignored gets less effective.** By the time I took over, the pattern was established in both directions.

## What I found

**I started with the business, not the team.** I worked through each product’s plans with product management and engineering and asked a plain question: what do you need from design and research this year, and what are you not getting? The answers were specific and mostly different from what the team had been doing.

**The gaps were in direction, not skill.** Each product needed someone who understood its roadmap and could do the research and design that roadmap depended on. What it had been getting was work chosen without that context.

## What I changed

**I directed the team around the needs and nothing else.** Each product got a designer or researcher as its lead, with a brief tied to what that product had said it needed. Work that did not serve a stated need stopped. New requests were measured against the needs already agreed, so the team was not pulled back into the old pattern by whoever asked most often.

**I managed through the leads and backed them.** Ownership was real: the lead decided the design and research direction for the product, and I supported those decisions when product management or engineering pushed back. That is also how people on the team grew, which is the part of management I care about most.

## What changed

**Product management and engineering started asking for the team’s work and using it.** That is the outcome I can state. It is a change in how the organization treated the team, and it is the reason I kept the arrangement.

**The structure went with me.** The product-lead arrangement, with a manager accountable for the whole portfolio and a lead accountable for each product, is the one I used to build the LinuxONE practice the following year.

## Evidence and limits

**The evidence is how the team was run and how the organization responded.** This page does not claim product results. Product telemetry for the AIOps portfolio belongs to those product teams, and the year was about making design and research useful to them, not about a single product decision.
